Verbs

TESTVerb count: one sentence = one main verb

Find the main verb. Is there already one?

Already has one?→ blank is -ing / to ___
✓The chef, hoping to impress the judges, adds saffron.
No main verb at all?→ blank is a real verb
✓The goal of the new study is simple.
TESTCover the middle

Cover everything between the subject and the blank. Swap in it or they.

The color of the leaves on these old trees changes in fall. → it changes
Subject = the noun before "of".  "each one", "every" → it.
Tense or verb form choices? → Card 6: Tense & verb form.

TESTFlip test

Flip it to "the ___ of the ___". the orbits of the planets → planets' orbits

TESTSwap test

Does "it is" or "they are" fit? Yes → it's / they're. No → its / their.

TESTPoint test

Point at the noun it means. One → it / its. Many → they / their. Skip the extra part in dashes.

4

Word order

TESTWho did it?

Opening phrase, → the first noun after the comma must be the one doing it.

✓Knowing the risk, the team waited.
✗Knowing the risk, the team's plan was to wait.
✗Knowing the risk, it was decided to wait.
Trap: an 's, "it", or "there" right after the comma.
"Compared to that of X," → that = the same kind of thing (a score, an arrangement). Start with that thing.

6

Tense & verb form

TESTJob test: what does the blank do?

Check helpers first. Then run Card 2's verb count in this clause. A who/that/which clause needs its own verb; and can join verbs.

has/have/had + past participle; is/was/been + -ing (doing) or past participle (receiving). Past participle = tested / written, not always -ed.
Main verb missing?→ tensed verb; a helper + verb counts as one group.
Already there, no connector?→ description: -ing for the doer; past participle for the receiver.
Purpose / preposition?→ to + verb = “in order to”; after by / without → -ing.
✓The researchers examining the pottery identified a new trade route.
Having + past participle is also a description; it cannot supply the main verb.
TESTTimeline test

Read time clues and verbs about the same event. In 1850 → past; currently → present; future → will + verb; future viewed from the past → would + verb. What a study or author says → usually present.

✓When the dam opened in 1936, it created Lake Mead.
TESTNow or then?

Past-to-now span (since / for, continuing now) → has/have + past participle. Completed before another past event (by the time) → had + past participle. A finished past period → simple past.

✓By the time the museum opened in 2010, the team had restored the murals.
TESTParallel test

Same job in a list or joined by and? Match the form; match tense when the time stays the same. A shared to or helper can carry across.

✓The team mapped the site, dated the tools, and cataloged the pottery.
