Quadratics

the #1 hard-module topic
"Exactly one", "no", or "two" solutions? → \(b^2-4ac\)

positive

2 solutions

zero

1 solution · "tangent"

negative

no real solutions

Example\(y=x^2+6x+k\) meets \(y=2\) once  →  \(x^2+6x+(k-2)=0\)  →  \(36-4(k-2)=0\)  →  \(k=11\)
Line + parabola? Set them equal first, then move everything to one side.

Standard

\(y=ax^2+bx+c\)
  • y-intercept \(=c\)
  • vertex at \(x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}\)

Factored

\(y=a(x-p)(x-q)\)
  • zeros: \(p\) and \(q\)
  • vertex: halfway between them

Vertex

\(y=a(x-h)^2+k\)
  • vertex \((h,\,k)\)
  • \((x+3)^2\) means \(h=-3\)

Sum & product

sum \(=-\dfrac ba\)   product \(=\dfrac ca\)

No solving needed.

Complete the square

\(x^2+bx=\left(x+\tfrac b2\right)^2-\left(\tfrac b2\right)^2\)

Half it, square it.

Won't factor?

\(x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\)

Up or down

\(a>0\): opens up, has a min

\(a<0\): opens down, has a max

Lines & systems

How many solutions? Compare the numbers.
Write both as \(Ax+By=C\).
\(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}\ne\dfrac{B_1}{B_2}\)one
\(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}=\dfrac{B_1}{B_2}\ne\dfrac{C_1}{C_2}\)none
all three equalinfinitely many

Slope

\(m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)

parallel: same \(m\)
perpendicular: flip & negate, \(\tfrac23\to-\tfrac32\)

Find \(x+y\)?

Add or subtract the two equations. Don't solve for each one.

One equation

\(ax+b=cx+d\)

same \(a\) & \(b\) → infinitely many
same \(a\) only → none

Inequality

× or ÷ by a negative → flip the sign.

Exponents & polynomials

Make the bases match.
Example\(8^x=2^{12}\) → \((2^3)^x=2^{3x}\) → \(3x=12\) → \(x=4\)
\(a^m\cdot a^n=a^{m+n}\)multiply → add \(\dfrac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}\)divide → subtract \((a^m)^n=a^{mn}\)power of power → multiply \(a^{-n}=\dfrac1{a^n}\)negative → flip \(a^{\frac mn}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}\)power over root

Remainder

\(p(x)\div(x-k)\) leaves \(p(k)\).

\(p(3)=0\) → \((x-3)\) is a factor.

Shift

\(f(x-3)+2\)

→ right 3, up 2

Absolute value

\(|u|=k\) → \(u=k\) or \(u=-k\)

"For all \(x\)"

Expand. Match the \(x^2\), \(x\), and number parts.

Circles

\((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\)
center \((h,k)\)radius \(r\)signs flip: \((x+3)^2\) → \(h=-3\)
Messy?\(x^2+6x+y^2-4y=12\) → \((x+3)^2+(y-2)^2=25\) → center \((-3,2)\), \(r=5\)
Complete the square on \(x\) and on \(y\); add the same numbers to the right side.

Slice of a circle

\(\dfrac{\text{angle}}{360^\circ}=\dfrac{\text{arc}}{2\pi r}=\dfrac{\text{sector}}{\pi r^2}\)

\(180^\circ=\pi\) radians

Tangent

Tangent line ⊥ radius at the touching point.

That's your right angle.

Statistics

Work with totals.
\(\text{total}=\text{mean}\times\text{count}\)
MedianPut in order. Take the middle.
OutlierPulls the mean, barely moves the median. Bigger spread → bigger SD.
Margin of errorMean 24 ± 3 → the population mean is likely 21 to 27.
Random…sample → can generalize  ·  assignment → can say "causes"
"Given that"Shrink the total to that row or column.
Best-fit linePredicted value = read the line, not the dot.

✓ Let Desmos do it

  • Solve anything: graph both sides, click where they cross.
  • Vertex / zeros: graph it, click the gray dots.
  • "Equivalent?" graph both; the right one sits on top.
  • "k is a constant": use a slider, then confirm with \(b^2-4ac\).
  • Stats: mean(L), median(L), stdev(L)

✋ Do it by hand

  • Answers with letters in them ("in terms of \(a\)").
  • Geometry pictures: similar triangles, circles, angles.
  • Exact answers like \(3\pm\sqrt7\) or \(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\).
  • "Which must be true" about samples and data.

✔ Quick checks

  • Plug-in test: letters in the choices? Pick \(x=2\), compute the original and each choice. Keep the match.
  • Try the choices: plug each answer back into the question.
  • Draw it: sketch the triangle or circle and label every number.
  • Does it make sense? A length can't be negative; a probability is 0 to 1.
